The Bell & GHZ games

Partners locked in separate rooms, no communication, free to agree on any plan beforehand. Classical plans hit a hard ceiling; shared entanglement walks through it.
Referee sends Alice x∈{0,1} and Bob y∈{0,1}. Each answers a bit. Win rule: a⊕b = x·y (agree, unless both questions are 1 — then disagree).

Team classical — design their plan

Alice answers, if asked x=0: Alice answers, if asked x=1: Bob answers, if asked y=0: Bob answers, if asked y=1:
All 16 plans fail ≥1 of the 4 question pairs: the win-conditions demand a0⊕b0=0, a0⊕b1=0, a1⊕b0=0, a1⊕b1=1 — the left sides sum to 0, the right to 1. Contradiction → max 75%.

Team quantum — one Bell pair per round

Alice measures at 0°/45° (x=0/1), Bob at 22.5°/−22.5° (y=0/1); each answers their outcome. P(agree) = cos²(angle difference) = cos²22.5° ≈ 0.854 for every question pair (and P(disagree)=0.854 exactly where the rule wants disagreement).
Stronger than ANY stored plan — Bell's theorem; confirmed loophole-free in 2015.

Now THREE players. The referee draws questions x,y,z with an even number of 1s — one of 000, 011, 101, 110. Each player answers a bit. Win rule: a⊕b⊕c = x∨y∨z (answers sum even for 000; odd for the rest).

All 64 plans fail ≥1 of the 4 triples: the win-conditions demand a0⊕b0⊕c0=0, a0⊕b1⊕c1=1, a1⊕b0⊕c1=1, a1⊕b1⊕c0=1 — left sides sum to 0 (every bit appears twice), right sides to 1. Contradiction → max 75%.

Team quantum — one GHZ triple per round

They share (|000⟩+|111⟩)/√2. Asked 0 → measure X; asked 1 → measure Y; answer your outcome. The GHZ state is an exact eigenstate: X·X·X = +1 and X·Y·Y = Y·X·Y = Y·Y·X = −1 — so the product of the three outcomes is FIXED for every allowed question triple, and it always lands on the win rule.
They win 100% — every round, deterministically. No statistics, no inequality: a single lost round would falsify quantum mechanics; a single 4/4 sweep is impossible classically. (Mermin 1990; realized experimentally by Pan et al. 2000.)
